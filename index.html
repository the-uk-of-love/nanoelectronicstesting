What we found

Every participant returned at least one positive reading

In the first study of its kind, 22 participants were each tested on two separate occasions using professional-grade electronics detection equipment — the same class of device security companies use to sweep for hidden bugs and transmitters. The equipment is built for one purpose: flagging the presence of synthetic silicon or microelectronic components, active or passive, regardless of what they're doing. Every participant returned a positive signal, with probability readings ranging from 10% to 100% across different locations on the body. The open question is what these signals mean, and where they come from.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is an NLJD scan?

A non-linear junction detector is a piece of equipment that identifies the electronic signature of semiconductor material — the kind of junction found in any electronic component, active or passive. It's the standard tool security professionals use to find hidden bugs and transmitters.

Is this a medical test?

No. This is not a medical diagnosis of any kind. It's an exploratory test using detection equipment, and any results are for personal information only.

What happens after my test?

You'll receive complete documentation of your results, including the readings taken at each location tested, for your own records.
